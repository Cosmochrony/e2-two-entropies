  \begin{abstract}
    The companion entanglement note and the orthogonality paper E1 identify two distinct
    entanglement entropies of the conjugate Weil pair $\{c,q-c\}$, which must not be
    conflated, and which now carry different epistemic status.
    The first is the entropy of the minimal admissible pair sector.
    The carrier of that sector is fixed by an explicit hypothesis, here labelled (H-carrier):
    the admissible sector of each factor is the atomic Fourier triple
    $\mathrm{span}\{e_0,e_{\xi_c},e_{q-\xi_c}\}$.
    The hypothesis is motivated but not derived.
    The pre-saturation pipeline proves that every selected Gram--Schmidt direction is a pure
    Fourier mode (\emph{Q5a-O2}), so an atomic carrier is the natural candidate; but the
    rank-three input is a supplied selection rule, not a derived constant (\emph{O23}), the
    pipeline's three-coordinate reduction is a truncation fixed in advance, and no mean-zero
    theorem removes the zero mode -- the passage to the pair sector is part of the sector
    definition.
    Under (H-carrier) --- which includes, as an explicit clause, the identification of the
    pair sector $\{e_{\xi_c},e_{q-\xi_c}\}$ with the spin-$\tfrac12$ module $V_{1/2}$ of the
    binary group acting in (H-inv) --- the pair sector carries the singlet analysis below.
    Under the further, independent hypothesis (H-inv) that the proto-state on this sector is
    invariant under the diagonal $2I$-action -- stated and left open, not derived, by \emph{Q3}
    -- the proto-state is the singlet, its reduced state is maximally mixed, and
    $S_{\mathrm{ent}}^{\mathrm{adm}}=\log 2$ for general canonical blocks.
    This admissible pair entropy is therefore conditional on both (H-carrier) and (H-inv), not
    unconditional.
    The second object is the full residual fibre-level rank $r_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)=R_\infty-R(n)$
    defined on the full Gram--Schmidt basis (rank $q$ at saturation, not the rank-3 admissible
    projection).
    For matched single-character blocks, E1 proves the diagonal Schmidt pairing and the flat
    residual spectrum, hence $S_{\mathrm{ent}}(n)=\log(r_{\mathrm{pair}}(n))$, under a supplied
    purity of the joint state, which E1 does not derive.
    For independently sampled canonical blocks, the same entropy rests on the supplied purity, on
    the residual admissibility indiscernibility hypothesis $[\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{res}}]$, and on
    extending \emph{O17}'s equal-residual-dimension result beyond its own toy model to the real
    admissibility pipeline.
    The metaplectic dilation $\phi_\omega$ is excluded as a bridge for $[\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{res}}]$
    because it acts between blocks, not within the residual support of one block.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
    \label{sec:intro}

    The companion entanglement note~\cite{Beau2026entnote} and the orthogonality paper
    E1~\cite{Beau2026ent1} establish results about the entanglement of a closed conjugate Weil
    pair $\{c,q-c\}$ in the spectral admissibility cascade.
    A careful reading shows that two genuinely different entanglement entropies are in play,
    attached to two different projections, with different epistemic status.
    Conflating them produces an apparent tension between a $\log 2$ figure and a depth-dependent
    $\log\rpair(n)$ figure that in fact rest on different hypotheses.

    This note separates them.
    The separation is not cosmetic: the two objects live on projections of different rank,
    have different physical roles, and have different epistemic status.
    One is the entropy of the minimal admissible sector, which is two-dimensional and carries
    the singlet relevant to diagonal-invariant bipartite structure; it holds for general blocks
    but only conditionally, under two independent hypotheses: (H-carrier), the two-clause
    atomic-carrier hypothesis fixing the admissible sector of each factor as the Fourier triple
    $\mathrm{span}\{e_0,e_{\xi_c},e_{q-\xi_c}\}$ and identifying its pair plane with the
    $2I$-module $V_{1/2}$, which no result of the corpus derives; and
    (H-inv), the diagonal-invariance hypothesis that \emph{Q3} states and leaves open.
    The other is the entropy of the full residual Gram--Schmidt rank, a finer pipeline-level
    observable; for matched blocks it is proved under a supplied purity of the joint state, which
    E1 does not derive, and otherwise it rests on bridge hypotheses of its own in addition.

  \section{Two distinct objects}
    \label{sec:objects}

    The O-series pipeline stores two distinct objects per conjugate pair, and
    \emph{Q5a-O2}~\cite{Beau2026q5ao2} (Remark 2.1) is explicit that they must not be identified.

    \begin{itemize}
      \item The full Gram--Schmidt basis is a unitary $q\times q$ matrix whose rows span all of
      $\mathbb{C}^q$ at saturation.
      Its cumulative admitted rank is $R(n)$, growing to $R_\infty=q$ at saturation, and
      the residual fibre-level rank is
      \[
        \rpair(n) = R_\infty - R(n),
      \]
      which is non-increasing in $n$.
      This is the object of the companion note~\cite{Beau2026entnote}.
      It is \emph{not} the theoretical admissible projection.
      \item The three-coordinate reduction of the pipeline projects onto the first three
      Gram--Schmidt directions $\{\phi_0,\phi_1,\phi_2\}$.
      \emph{Q5a-O2}~\cite{Beau2026q5ao2} (Remark 2.1 and Proposition 4.1) establishes that this
      reduction is a truncation whose retained dimension is fixed in advance, not a canonical
      projection, and that no quantity computed from it can measure the admissible rank.
      The rank-3 admissible pair carrier used in Section~\ref{sec:adm} is therefore introduced
      there as an explicit hypothesis, not imported as a theorem.
    \end{itemize}

    We therefore distinguish the admissible pair entropy $\Sadm$, defined on $\Pi_q$
    (Section~\ref{sec:adm}), from the full residual-rank entropy $\Sent(n)$, defined on the
    full Gram--Schmidt basis (Section~\ref{sec:residual}).

  \section{The admissible pair sector: \texorpdfstring{$\Sadm=\log 2$}{S=log 2} under (H-carrier) and (H-inv)}
    \label{sec:adm}

    The admissible sector is not derived from the corpus.
    Its structure is fixed here by an explicit carrier hypothesis, stated once and carried
    through every downstream statement.

    \begin{quote}
      \textbf{(H-carrier)} \emph{(hypothesis of the present note; derived nowhere in the
      corpus.)}
      For each prime $q$ and each conjugate pair $(c, q-c)$: (i) the admissible sector of
      each factor is the atomic Fourier triple
      \[
        \ran(\Pi_q^{(c)}) = \mathrm{span}\{e_0,\, e_{\xi_c},\, e_{q-\xi_c}\},
      \]
      with $e_\xi(k)=q^{-1/2}e^{2\pi i\xi k/q}$ and $\xi_c\in\{1,\dots,\lfloor q/2\rfloor\}$;
      and (ii) the pair-sector plane $\mathrm{span}\{e_{\xi_c}, e_{q-\xi_c}\}$ carries the
      spin-$\tfrac12$ representation $V_{1/2}$ of the binary group whose diagonal action
      enters (H-inv), i.e.\ the module identification $\mathcal{A}_c \cong V_{1/2}$ is part
      of the hypothesis.
    \end{quote}

    Three facts delimit the status of (H-carrier).
    First, it is structurally motivated: every selected Gram--Schmidt direction of the
    pre-saturation pipeline is a pure Fourier mode (\emph{Q5a-O2}~\cite{Beau2026q5ao2},
    Propositions 3.1--3.2; equivalently the orthogonality result of
    E1~\cite{Beau2026ent1}), and conjugate frequencies enter as parity partners, so a
    three-dimensional parity-closed admissible sector is necessarily a triple of the stated
    form.
    Second, the rank-three input is not derived: the threshold $\Sigma_c(n_3)=3$ is a supplied
    selection rule (\emph{O23}~\cite{Beau2026a27}, whose adjoint-dimension theorem concerns a
    supplied spinor carrier and leaves both the carrier selection and the identification of the
    capacity observable open, within the fibre-identification problem of
    \emph{O18}~\cite{Beau2026a22}); and the pipeline's three-coordinate reduction is a
    truncation whose outputs cannot measure the rank
    (\emph{Q5a-O2}~\cite{Beau2026q5ao2}, Proposition 4.1).
    Third, no mean-zero theorem removes the zero mode: $e_0$ belongs to the hypothesised
    carrier itself, so the passage to the pair sector below is part of the sector definition
    --- the parity-pair component of the triple --- not the consequence of a vanishing-mean
    lemma.
    No numerical support-size table is invoked: the archived pipeline diagnostics are not
    reproducible from the published repositories and include a delocalised case
    ($q=61$, pair $c=2$).

    Under (H-carrier), the pair sector of each factor is the two-dimensional
    conjugate-frequency pair (named the Born--Infeld parity pair after the candidate parity
    structure of the programme; the name imports no fibre claim)
    \[
      \mathcal{A}_c
      := \mathrm{span}\{e_{\xi_c}, e_{q-\xi_c}\},
      \qquad
      q-\xi_c \equiv -\xi_c \pmod q,
    \]
    which carries the spin-$\tfrac12$ representation $V_{1/2}\cong\mathbb{C}^2$ by clause
    (ii) of (H-carrier).
    The module identification is a genuine further assumption, not a consequence of the
    Fourier structure: the natural involution of the plane
    $\mathrm{span}\{e_{\xi_c}, e_{q-\xi_c}\}$ is the swap $e_{\xi_c} \leftrightarrow
    e_{q-\xi_c}$, with eigenvalues $\pm 1$, whereas the central element $-1 \in 2I$ must
    act as $-\mathbf{1}$ on $V_{1/2}$, so no canonical $2I$-action on the plane is supplied
    by the Weil structure.
    The two factors of the conjugate pair $\{c,q-c\}$ each carry a copy of $\mathcal{A}_c$,
    and the bipartite proto-state lives on $\mathcal{A}_c\otimes\mathcal{A}_c$.

    \begin{quote}
      \textbf{(H-inv)} \emph{(Q3~\cite{Beau2026q3}, hypothesis, not a theorem of that paper.)}
      The proto-state $|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{A}_c\otimes\mathcal{A}_c$ is invariant under the
      diagonal $2I$-action: for every $g\in 2I$, $(D(g)\otimes D(g))|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle$.
    \end{quote}

    \emph{Q3} states (H-inv) as an explicit hypothesis and proves what it buys once assumed; it
    does not derive (H-inv) from Born--Infeld indiscernibility, from admissibility, or from any
    other structural principle, and says so explicitly.
    Whether a physically admissible bipartite proto-state must satisfy (H-inv) is a separate,
    open question.
    The present note imports (H-inv) as a hypothesis on the same footing as \emph{Q3}, rather
    than deriving it.

    \begin{theorem}[Admissible pair entropy under (H-carrier) and (H-inv)]
      \label{thm:adm}
      Assume (H-carrier) and (H-inv).
      Then the proto-state on $\mathcal{A}_c\otimes\mathcal{A}_c$ is the singlet
      \[
        |\Omega_c\rangle
        = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
        \bigl(|\xi_c\rangle\otimes|q-\xi_c\rangle
        - |q-\xi_c\rangle\otimes|\xi_c\rangle\bigr).
      \]
      Its reduced state is $\rho^{\mathrm{adm}}_c = \tfrac12\mathbf{1}_2$, and hence
      \[
        \Sadm = \log 2.
      \]
    \end{theorem}

    \begin{proof}
      By clauses (i) and (ii) of (H-carrier), the pair sector of each factor is
      $\mathcal{A}_c\cong V_{1/2}$ as a $2I$-module.
      By \emph{Q3}~\cite{Beau2026q3}, Theorem 3.2, applied to $j=\tfrac12$: the invariants of the
      diagonal $2I$-action on $V_{1/2}\otimes V_{1/2}$ form a one-dimensional space (Schur's
      lemma, via $V_{1/2}\otimes V_{1/2}=V_0\oplus V_1$ and irreducibility/self-duality of
      $V_{1/2}$), spanned by the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ singlet $|\Omega_c\rangle$.
      Under (H-inv), the proto-state lies in this space and, being normalised, equals
      $|\Omega_c\rangle$ up to phase.
      The Schmidt decomposition of $|\Omega_c\rangle$ has two terms with equal coefficients
      $1/\sqrt 2$, so $\rho^{\mathrm{adm}}_c = \tfrac12\mathbf{1}_2$ and
      $\Sadm = -\mathrm{Tr}(\tfrac12\mathbf{1}_2\log\tfrac12\mathbf{1}_2) = \log 2$.
    \end{proof}

    Theorem~\ref{thm:adm} uses only the admissible parity pair $\{e_{\xi_c},e_{q-\xi_c}\}$,
    fixed by (H-carrier) for any block parameters, so it does not require a canonical
    identification of the full residual supports.
    But it does depend on two independent hypotheses, neither derived here nor elsewhere in
    the corpus: (H-carrier), which fixes the admissible pair sector to be two-dimensional at
    all, and (H-inv), which fixes the proto-state on that sector to be the singlet
    (\emph{Q3}'s own condition on its uniqueness theorem).
    The admissible pair entropy is $\log 2$ for general canonical blocks \emph{given both},
    not unconditionally.

  \section{The full residual rank: matched blocks and \texorpdfstring{$\Hres$}{[H res]}}
    \label{sec:residual}

    The full residual fibre-level rank $\rpair(n)=R_\infty-R(n)$ is a different object,
    defined on the full Gram--Schmidt basis rather than on $\Pi_q$.

    On matched single-character blocks, E1~\cite{Beau2026ent1} (Theorem~7) proves, under a
    supplied purity of the joint state, that the residual Schmidt spectrum is flat and that
    \[
      \Sent(n) = \log \rpair(n).
    \]
    The proof uses the sign-reflected Fourier supports $S_{q-c}^{\mathrm{res}}(n)
  = -S_c^{\mathrm{res}}(n)$, which canonically identify the residual modes of the two factors;
    given purity, the conjugation identity then selects the diagonal Schmidt pairing and the
    maximally entangled residual state.
    E1 does not derive that purity: it exhibits the maximally entangled state as the natural
    construction compatible with the Fourier-mode structure, and states that the purity clause
    remains a supplied composition structure.
    The matched-block result is therefore a theorem conditional on supplied purity, not an
    unconditional one.

    For independently sampled canonical blocks, E1 also shows that this literal mode-by-mode
    identification is not automatic.
    \emph{O17}~\cite{Beau2026a21} supplies equality of the residual dimensions of the two
    factors, but not a canonical pairing -- and only within O17's own scalar toy model, for
    an arbitrary pair of blocks: O17's own scope section states that no argument in that paper
    connects its toy model to the real admissibility pipeline, so its equal-dimension result is
    not yet shown to transfer to the conjugate-pair setting used here.
    This is carried below as a standing gap alongside $\Hres$, not folded silently into it.
    Universal admission orthogonality ($w_j=1$, E1 Theorem~4) ensures each factor has an
    orthonormal residual basis, but orthonormality and equal dimension do not by themselves
    fix the joint state or force a flat marginal.

    \begin{definition}[Residual admissibility indiscernibility $\Hres$]
      \label{def:hres}
      At BFS depth $n$, the residual fingerprint directions of a conjugate Weil factor are
      residually admissibility-indiscernible if no invariant carried by the admissible
      generators distinguishes them, equivalently if assigning unequal weights would constitute
      a premature admissible resolution of the open residual fibre.
    \end{definition}

    \begin{proposition}[Conditional flatness]
      \label{prop:hres}
      Assume the closure of the conjugate pair as a pure object, assume $\Hres$, and assume that
      \emph{O17}'s equal-residual-dimension result transfers from its own toy model to the real
      admissibility pipeline for conjugate pairs (open; not established by O17 itself).
      Then the reduced state on the residual support is
      $\rho_c^{\mathrm{res}}(n) = \rpair(n)^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{\rpair(n)}$, and
      $\Sent(n)=\log\rpair(n)$ for general canonical blocks.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      If unequal weights on the residual directions were admissibility-visible, they would
      define a decomposition of the open residual fibre that is stable under the admissible
      generators, i.e.\ a premature splitting, which is forbidden by Axiom A3~\cite{Beau2026m}
      and the non-factorisability lemma of the Foundation paper prior to projection locking.
      Under $\Hres$, no admissibility-visible invariant distinguishes the residual directions.
      Therefore the admissible reduced state cannot assign different weights to them without
      introducing extra structure not present in the admissibility data: the only
      admissibility-invariant reduced state is proportional to the identity.
      Together with closure (pure joint state) and the equal residual dimensions assumed to
      transfer from \emph{O17}~\cite{Beau2026a21}'s toy model, the reduced state is
      $\rho_c^{\mathrm{res}}(n)=\rpair(n)^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{\rpair(n)}$, giving
      $\Sent(n)=\log\rpair(n)$.
    \end{proof}

    \begin{remark}[The dilation does not supply $\Hres$]
      \label{rem:dilation}
      The metaplectic dilation $\phi_\omega(a,b,z)=(\omega a,\omega^{-1}b,z)$ of
      \emph{O31}~\cite{Beau2026a35} forces $\sigma_c(n)=\sigma_{\omega c}(n)$, relating the block
      of character $c$ to the block of character $\omega c$.
      It acts between blocks, not within the residual support of a single block, and so does not
      permute the residual modes of one factor.
      It cannot make the residual modes admissibility-indiscernible, and is therefore not a
      bridge for $\Hres$.
      $\Hres$ has a status parallel to the conditional hypotheses [H-color] and [H-ext] elsewhere
      in the programme.
    \end{remark}

  \section{Status}
    \label{sec:status}

    E1~\cite{Beau2026ent1} proves universal admission orthogonality: each admitted $k{=}3$ Weil
    fingerprint is a Fourier mode, and a newly admitted frequency is orthogonal to the previous
    span.
    \emph{O17}~\cite{Beau2026a21} proves equality of residual dimensions within its own toy
    model, for an arbitrary pair of blocks; extending this to the conjugate-pair setting of the
    real admissibility pipeline is open (Section~\ref{sec:residual}).

    The admissible pair carrier is hypothesised, not derived: (H-carrier) fixes the sector of
    each factor as the atomic Fourier triple and, as an explicit clause, identifies its
    parity-pair component $\{e_{\xi_c},e_{q-\xi_c}\}$ with the $2I$-module $V_{1/2}$.
    Its structural support is the pure-mode form of the selected pipeline directions
    (\emph{Q5a-O2}, Propositions 3.1--3.2) and the parity pairing of conjugate frequencies;
    its rank-three input is a supplied selection rule (\emph{O23}), the pipeline's
    three-coordinate reduction being a truncation from which no rank can be read
    (\emph{Q5a-O2}, Proposition 4.1).
    No mean-zero theorem and no reproducible support-size numerics back the carrier: the zero
    mode belongs to the hypothesised triple, and the archived support diagnostics are not
    reproducible and include a delocalised case.
    What is \emph{not} hypothesised away, on top of (H-carrier), is the proto-state on this
    sector: Theorem~\ref{thm:adm} identifies it as the singlet, and hence
    \[
      \Sadm = \log 2,
    \]
    only under the further, independent hypothesis (H-inv) that \emph{Q3}~\cite{Beau2026q3}
    states and leaves open, not under any unconditional forcing.
    Neither (H-carrier) nor (H-inv) is derived from Born--Infeld structure, from
    admissibility, or from any other principle in the present corpus.

    By contrast, the full residual-rank statement
    \[
      \Sent(n) = \log \rpair(n)
    \]
    is proved for matched single-character blocks (E1 Theorem 7) only under a supplied purity of
    the joint state, which E1 does not derive.
    For independently sampled canonical blocks it rests on that supplied purity together with
    $\Hres$, the residual admissibility indiscernibility hypothesis (Definition~\ref{def:hres}),
    and on extending \emph{O17}'s equal-dimension result to the real pipeline
    (Section~\ref{sec:residual}).
    The metaplectic dilation is excluded as a bridge for $\Hres$ (Remark~\ref{rem:dilation}).

    The separation is therefore:
    \begin{align*}
      \text{admissible pair sector, under (H-carrier), (H-inv)}
      &\;\Longrightarrow\;
      \Sadm = \log 2,\\
      \text{residual-rank sector, matched blocks,}\\
      \text{under a supplied purity}
      &\;\Longrightarrow\;
      \Sent(n) = \log \rpair(n).
    \end{align*}
    The first holds for general canonical blocks, but only conditionally on (H-carrier) and
    (H-inv) jointly.
    The second holds for matched single-character blocks under a supplied purity of the joint
    state, which E1 does not derive, and otherwise only conditionally, on that purity together
    with $\Hres$ and the O17 transfer gap.
    Neither entropy is established unconditionally.
